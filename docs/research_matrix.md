Search/inspection date: **2026-10-25 UTC**. This is a bounded implementation-oriented review, not a systematic review. No `persistent_affect_research.md` or corresponding PDF was present in the checked workspace. All eleven user seed URLs were opened successfully. We distinguish what a publication reports from independently reproduced findings; none of the external experiments below was reproduced here.

## Novelty constraints and research consequence

**Established precedent:** appraisal, coping/reappraisal, multiple timescales, homeostatic motivation, external persistent affect, affect-biased memory, learned memory weights, learning progress, divergent/convergent stages and functional emotion representations in LLMs.

**Engineering synthesis:** a durable, evidence-preserving, replayable software loop combining these pieces. Combining them is useful engineering, not by itself defensible proof of architectural novelty.

**Speculative hypothesis:** an outcome-trained regulation mechanism may improve switching between idea exploration and verification, and learned history-sensitive appraisal may help transfer after setbacks. The plausible departure is a specific coupled computational mechanism and its causal test, not the presence of emotion words. Assess against both an identically computed controller with labels removed and a structurally different ordinary adaptive controller with matched information/actions/budget.

**Measured result:** reserved for this repository's executed experiments. External authors' reported results above are not measurements made here. A synthetic/controller gain cannot be promoted to a live-LLM creativity gain.

## Verification gaps and follow-up

The complete technical Anthropic paper exceeded the browser content limit; the primary report and its stated experimental limitations were read. Several recent architectures were checked at abstract/metadata level only, so fine-grained “first mechanism” claims remain unsupported. Code links not located should be revisited before publication. No human longitudinal affect annotations, physiological data, or independent participant study was available. Nothing in this review establishes subjective experience or biological fidelity. The most useful literature follow-up is driven by a measured causal ambiguity: if regulation improves executable task results, compare its state/features and training objective in detail with ordinary metareasoning and intrinsic-motivation methods before describing the result as affect-specific.
